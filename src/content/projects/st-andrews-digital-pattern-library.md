The **Digital Pattern Library** (DPL) is the design system behind the University of St Andrews’ websites and apps. I was its lead developer in the Digital Communications team, which looks after the university’s external web presence.

## The problem

A university website serves a lot of people with very different needs: prospective students, current students, staff, researchers, and every department with its own content. Those departments need to build pages that look and behave consistently, are accessible, and fit the constraints of the CMS. At the same time, the design team needs its intent to survive contact with real content.

## What I did

- **Rebuilt it around students.** I evolved the DPL so its components centred on the university’s most important audience. This most often meant challenging intent among the Schools & departments-- Yes, we have a fantastic library with N number of accolades, but the students are most often looking for the open hours!
- **Accessible, documented components.** I built reusable components with documentation, balancing design intent with CMS constraints, and they were adopted across departments.
- **Faster builds.** I cut the library’s build time from about **1.5 minutes to about 20 seconds**, so iterating on components stopped being a chore.
- **Running a platform.** I also managed a WordPress multisite of **180+ sites**.

## How we worked

The team ran a mature agile (DSDM) process with designers and communications stakeholders, and I mentored junior developers.

## Why it matters now

This is where I learned that a design system is a product with its own users. Here they were the developers, designers and content editors across the University who build with it. That idea has shaped everything since, including the [LiveOps platform](/work/outplay-liveops-platform/) at Outplay.
