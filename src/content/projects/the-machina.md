## The books

> "Science fiction from a machine intelligence view."

The Machina: Refugees of Darterra, and the Novella Arcology, tell the stories of a race of sentient machines, forced from their home, and their intersection with humanity on Earth.

## Why it’s on a developer’s portfolio

A delightful non-code project. Writing, and finishing, any written work is far harder than getting the first draft onto the page. And the Second draft. The Final_version_4b. 

And then you have the uniquely painful, but very necessary, process of editing. Of taking the carefully crafted vignettes, the maybe too flowery description of the woods, and asking yourself, 

> "Does this belong? Does it help me tell the story I am trying to tell?". 

Or having a trusted friend, or spouse (thanks Rebecca!), help edit. To have the outside-of-the-story eye say, "This is nice, but I don't get it..."

Thankfully the process of publishing was very straightforward for me; I was writing with Scrivener, and compiling an ebook for Amazon sure beats getting C++ to compile on Windows.

## The audiobook

Quite simply I met another Aaron (Smith) at Outplay Entertainment, who happened to be doing a side business of narrating audiobooks. And me being the only author at Outplay at that time-- we got a mystery writer a later year!-- it was actually a really good fit.

I had not considered all the ways in which people, and thus Aaron Smith, might mis-pronounce or be unable to pronounce some of the names in the Machina. 

> Ma-key-naw. Not Ma-shi-na. Haha. 

I am delighted with how Aaron narrated the story, and am beyond chuffed that I got a story of mine read like that.
