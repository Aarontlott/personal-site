A creative break from the day job, and my first ever print project.

If you played **The Witcher 3**, you probably lost a worrying number of hours to Gwent, the card game inside the game. Lots of people printed their own decks before the official cards came out. My twist: every card would star one of my coworkers, paired with a piece of art that looked like them (or at least captured them).

<!-- ![Five Gwent cards from The Witcher 3: an illustrated character at the top, with a name and quote on a parchment texture below. The cards are tall and narrow.](/images/gwent/gwent-cards.jpg) -->

## Getting “volunteers”

I meant to do a handful. Then I got into the right mood for it and suddenly I was placing an order for **80 cards**: the whole team at my workplace at the time, plus my old workplace back in Scotland.

> It wasn’t really volunteers, to be exact.

People supplied their own quotes where they could. For everyone else, I went digging through Goodreads Quotes, which had a great selection.

## Design decisions

I didn’t copy the traditional Gwent layout, and not only to make it my own:

- **300 dpi for print.** Most of the available art wasn’t high enough resolution for full-bleed Gwent-style cards at that density, so the design had to work around it.
- **Tarot sizing.** Bigger and more satisfying in the hand than a standard trading card.
- **Gold gilt edging.** It doubled the price of the decks. It was worth it.

## The hard part: picking the art

Choosing an image for each person was the toughest job. Knowing the lore behind the characters, I didn’t want to saddle anyone with somebody evil or unpleasant.

> Except for one of the designers. He wears all black, so he got Eredin.

The whole project started with one image. While idly thinking about the idea, I found a piece of art that looked *uncannily* like my colleague Ben. When I showed him his card, he was a little taken aback. This was very much out of left field.

<!-- ![Art of a bald man talking his way out of trouble with some guards. Ben in real life is well spoken and looks eerily like this.](/images/gwent/ben-art.png) -->

The rest came together over a few weeks of early mornings before work.

## How it landed

Better than I could have hoped. It wasn’t an office of card-game nerds, and most people hadn’t held a trading card since Pokémon (if ever). They were still a universal hit.
